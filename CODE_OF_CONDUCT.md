# Code of conduct

## Our pledge

We want participation in this project to be a good experience for everyone,
regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex
characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race,
religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Our standards

Behaviour that builds a constructive, welcoming environment:

- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback. This project reviews
  its own work hard, and "that's wrong" is usually a gift here — the a11y
  audit, for example, was worth running precisely because it found things
  nobody was looking for.
- Accepting responsibility, apologising to those affected by our mistakes, and
  learning from the experience.
- Focusing on what is best for the community and the people using the tool.
- Being precise rather than loud. "This is wrong because X" beats "this is
  nonsense" every time.

Behaviour that is not acceptable:

- Sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political
  attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address,
  without their explicit permission.
- Dismissing or bad-faith re-litigating a decision the maintainer has already
  made. If you want a decision revisited, make a case for it; do not
  repeatedly re-raise it. See the scope note below.
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces — the repository, its
issues, pull requests, and discussions — and also applies when an individual
is officially representing the project in public spaces.

## Reporting

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behaviour may be
reported to the maintainer. See [`SECURITY.md`](./SECURITY.md) for how to
reach the maintainer privately. All complaints will be reviewed and
investigated promptly and fairly, and the privacy and security of the reporter
will be respected.

## A note on scope decisions

ferry is a focused post-production tool, and the maintainer makes scope calls
that contributors do not get to overrule. The clearest current example:
**screen-reader support has been explicitly declined**, so issues requesting a
VoiceOver or NVDA pass will be closed without further discussion. That is a
decision, not an oversight, and it is not up for re-argument in a thread.

Disagreeing with a scope decision is completely fine — open an issue and make
the case. What is not fine is re-raising a settled decision repeatedly. One
well-argued issue is welcome. The fifth identical issue is not.

Accessibility work that is not about speech output — focus order, labelling,
contrast, keyboard traps — remains in scope and is genuinely welcome.
